Our purpose

The Cobbs Creek Community Greening Plan is a community-led strategic framework for revitalizing Cobbs Creek Park, including its trails, parkland, and creek. Developed with residents and local organizations, the plan places community knowledge and priorities at the center of decisions about the park's future.

City agencies, including the Philadelphia Water Department and Streets Department, and community organizations have projects underway or funded in the surrounding area. These efforts represent an opportunity for coordinated investment, but they are not yet aligned around a shared vision. The plan will connect this work through a common set of resident-defined priorities, identifying where collaboration can strengthen existing projects and address unmet needs.

The planning process will also establish an ongoing partnership among local organizations, strengthening their collective capacity to guide implementation and sustain progress beyond the plan's completion. Although the plan does not fund or carry out construction, it will establish a clear sequence of priorities for future projects and investment. It will identify what should happen, where, and in what order, providing a foundation for directing resources toward the improvements residents have identified as most important.

How the project takes shape

The area it covers

The project focuses on Cobbs Creek Park, its trails, and the creek, spanning the Cobbs Creek and Eastwick neighborhoods of West and Southwest Philadelphia.

Who decides

A Steering Committee of residents and local organizations guides the planning process and selects the consultant. Committee members developed the criteria used to evaluate consultant proposals.

Residents also participate as Community Greening Liaisons, bringing local knowledge, lived experience, and trusted community relationships to inform the plan and connect the process with their neighbors.

Nominations for Community Greening Liaisons are open through November 16.

What it produces

The project will produce a published Community Greening Plan with an implementation framework that establishes priorities and next steps. It will identify four to eight specific funding and programming opportunities to advance those priorities.

The process will also establish an ongoing partnership among local organizations, strengthening their collective capacity to pursue opportunities, coordinate implementation, and sustain progress beyond the planning phase.

The six Greening Plan priorities

Community input to date has identified six priorities for Cobbs Creek Park, its trails, and the creek. These priorities provide a starting point for the planning process and reflect areas of alignment with City agencies. Working with residents and the Steering Committee, the consultant will refine them into short-, medium-, and long-term goals, with clear steps for advancing specific projects.

Priorities labeled “Shared agency priority” also align with those of Philadelphia Parks & Recreation (PPR) or the Philadelphia Water Department (PWD).

Priority 01

Park, creek and trail restoration and stewardship

Restoring and protecting the ecological health of Cobbs Creek, the park, and surrounding communities is the foundation for every other priority. Healthy woodlands, wetlands, waterways, and trails are essential to the park's long-term resilience.

Priority 02

Communications, internal and external

Open, transparent communication among residents, organizations, agencies, and stakeholders will strengthen trust and collaboration throughout the planning process.

Shared agency priority
Priority 03

Programming and education

Educational programming should connect residents to the park through environmental education, wellness, history, recreation, and volunteer opportunities.

Priority 04

Forming a resident-led park organization

A strong resident-led organization can provide long-term stewardship, local accountability, and meaningful community leadership.

Shared agency priority
Priority 05

Environmental justice and park resource advocacy

Park improvements should benefit existing residents while helping preserve the character and diversity of surrounding neighborhoods.

Priority 06

Maintenance and illegal dumping

Consistent maintenance and addressing illegal dumping are critical to protecting the park, improving public safety, and preserving community pride.

Shared agency priority
